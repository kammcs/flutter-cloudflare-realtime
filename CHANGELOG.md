# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **System calls on Android: Telecom through Core-Telecom** (`docs/design.md` §4.8, roadmap M11). On Android 8+ `SystemCalls.instance.configure()` now completes with `true`, and calls live in Telecom:
  - Every method and event of the native contract: incoming and outgoing calls (`CallsManager.addCall`), answer, end with a reason (Telecom's disconnect causes both ways), hold and unhold (`setInactive` / `setActive`), connected, and the system's mute (the global microphone mute, which Telecom, cars and watches set), each reported once per real change whether the app or the system acted. Call audio routes through Telecom's endpoints (`requestEndpointChange`); their UUIDs are the route IDs.
  - `CallService` runs with the type `phoneCall` while a call exists (plus `microphone` and `camera` as rooms publish), and its notification is the call's `CallStyle` one: ringing on a new high-importance "Incoming calls" channel (the ringtone, a full-screen intent, Answer and Decline), then ongoing with Hang up. Answer opens the app's launch activity, which answers and shows over the lock screen until the call ends.
  - The manifest adds `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_PHONE_CALL`, `USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT`, `phoneCall` on `CallService` and the notification actions' receiver; Core-Telecom 1.0.1 (Apache-2.0) adds `MANAGE_OWN_CALLS` and its `ConnectionService`. New overridable strings for the notification. README: "System calls on Android".
  - Example: **Start as system call** and **Simulate incoming call** on the join screen, a hold button in the call, and the notification and full-screen permissions. `integration_test/system_call_test.dart` (with `system_call_test_driver.sh` on Android): outgoing and incoming calls in a room, mute both ways, hold, Telecom endpoints, the notification's Answer, Decline and Hang up, and a call reported from the background.

- **System calls: CallKit and Android Telecom, the Dart side** (`docs/design.md` §4.8, roadmap M11). The native backends come next; until then phones report `supported == false` and behave like desktops.
  - `SystemCalls.instance`: `configure(SystemCallsConfig)` (completes with `supported`), `reportIncomingCall(...)` and `startOutgoingCall(...)` (a UUID `id`, a `CallHandle`, `displayName`, `video`, a push `payload`), `calls` / `callsChanges`, and `events`: `SystemCallAddedEvent`, `AnsweredEvent`, `EndedEvent` (with a `SystemCallEndReason`), `HeldEvent`, `MutedEvent`, `DtmfEvent`, `AudioActivatedEvent` and `AudioDeactivatedEvent`, whether the app or the system's UI (the lock screen, a headset, a car) acted.
  - `SystemCall`: `state` (`ringing`, `dialing`, `connecting`, `active`, `held`, `ended`), `muted`, `endReason`, `whenEnded`, and the requests `answer()`, `end([reason])`, `setHeld()`, `setMuted()`, `reportConnecting()`, `reportConnected()` and `update()`. Requests complete once the system accepts them; the state changes when it confirms. A refusal throws `SystemCallException` (`SystemCallErrorCode`).
  - `SystemCalls.instance.voipPush` (iOS PushKit): `register()`, `unregister()`, `token` / `tokenChanges`. The package will report a pushed call to CallKit natively, as iOS requires; the push's payload carries the call.
  - `Room.attachSystemCall(call)`: the system's mute button and the room's microphone stay in step both ways (muting wins when they disagree), the room leaves when the call ends, and leaving ends the call (`leaveWhenEnded`, `endWhenLeft`); `Room.systemCall`, `detachSystemCall()`.
  - While a system call exists, call audio follows the system: the platform leaves call mode, routes come from Telecom's endpoints on Android (the route API and policy are unchanged; a picked route carries over by kind), and holds interrupt the call's audio with the new `CallInterruptionReason.held`; `resumeAudio()` takes the call off hold.
  - Desktops, the web and phones without the system's support keep the calls in Dart, answering every request with its event, so an app's call flow is the same everywhere.

- **Fixed: a publish long after joining failed with `SessionGoneException`** (410, "Session appears to be disconnected"). The SFU expires a session whose peer connection never connected, about ten seconds after creating it, and nothing connected until the first publish, so a permission prompt, Android's screen-capture consent, the iOS broadcast picker or a slow first capture was enough (roadmap M10; `docs/design.md` §8.1, "Publishing late"):
  - **The room connects its session at join** (and after a re-session that moved nothing), by setting up the session's DataChannel transport. `RoomOptions.connectEarly` (default `true`) turns it off. A room is now `connecting` right after join, until the peer connection connects.
  - **A publish whose session fails under it is retried once** on the room's new session (`publishCamera`, `publishMicrophone`, `publishScreen`, `publishMediaSource`, `room.data.publish` and `subscribe`), and its future completes normally. A second failure, a re-session that gives up, or reconnection being off still throws.
  - `SfuSession.establishConnection()` connects a session before anything is published, for apps that use `SfuSession` directly; `SfuSession.hasNegotiated` says whether an SDP exchange has completed.
  - `SfuSession.connect` creates the peer connection while `sessions/new` runs instead of after it (flutter_webrtc's first peer connection can take seconds on a loaded machine), and releases whichever was created when the other fails.
  - Example: `integration_test/late_publish_test.dart` (joins, idles 30 s, publishes, checks frames; with and without `connectEarly`). `sfu_loopback_test.dart` connects its sessions early.
- **Fixed: a late publish retried on a new session was sometimes never pulled** by other participants (`connectEarly: false`; seen in `late_publish_test` on a Pixel). The retried push was the new session's first negotiation, and the SFU sometimes never serves a track pushed before its session connected (every pull answers `not_found_track_error`). The re-session now connects its new session before a waiting publish runs on it, as `connectEarly` does at join (`docs/design.md` §8.1, Publishing late).
- **Fixed: DataChannels on a fresh session failed on Android** since the RTX repair below: the repair reads the local description before applying an SFU offer, and Android's plugin throws for a peer connection that has none yet (the first `datachannels/establish`). It now counts as no local description.
- **Calls that survive the phone** (`docs/design.md` §4.7, roadmap M10 part B):
  - **Background on Android:** while a room publishes a microphone or a capturing camera, the package runs a foreground service (`CallService`) of type `microphone`, plus `camera` while the camera captures, so the call keeps its microphone, camera and connection in the background. It starts on the first publish, follows the camera, and stops when nothing is published or the room is left. Its manifest entries merge into the app (`FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MICROPHONE`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_CAMERA`, `WAKE_LOCK`); the notification's texts and icon are overridable resources. `RoomOptions.foregroundService: false` turns it off for apps with their own service. A failed start is a `RoomErrorEvent('foregroundService', …)`, retried in the foreground. It is separate from the screen share's `mediaProjection` service.
  - **Background on iOS:** with the app's `UIBackgroundModes: audio` the call keeps its audio. The camera, which iOS stops in the background, is reported: `Room.cameraPause` / `cameraPauseChanges` / `canDetectCameraPause`, `LocalCameraPausedEvent(CameraPauseReason)` and `LocalCameraResumedEvent`.
  - **Interruptions** on both phones: `CallInterruptedEvent(CallInterruptionReason)` and `CallResumedEvent`, `Room.audioInterruption` / `audioInterruptionChanges` / `canDetectAudioInterruptions`, and `Room.resumeAudio()`. iOS's audio session interruptions and Android's audio focus loss (`phoneCall` or `otherAudio`; iOS says `unknown`). While interrupted the call is silent both ways, without announcing a mute; it resumes when the platform gives the audio back or the app returns to the foreground.
  - **The proximity sensor** turns the screen off at the ear during a voice call on the earpiece (off on the speaker, a headset and with video): `RoomOptions.proximitySensor` (default `true`), `Room.proximitySensorActive` / `proximitySensorChanges`.
  - Example: `integration_test/background_test.dart` (with `background_test_driver.sh` driving Home and back over adb on Android), and test hooks in the example's `MainActivity` (taking the audio focus, listing foreground services).
- **Fixed: a video push after pulling a video failed** with "Failed to set remote video description send parameters for m-section with mid=...". Seen pushing an iPhone screen share after the microphone with a camera already pulled; reproduced on macOS. Once a session has pulled a video, the SFU renumbers the pushed codec in its answer but leaves the RTX `apt` at the offered number, which libwebrtc rejects. The session now repairs the `apt` in every SFU answer and offer before applying it (`docs/design.md` §4.2).
- **Screen share on iOS** (`docs/design.md` §10, roadmap M9):
  - `ScreenShareSource.start()` and `LocalParticipant.publishScreen()` work on iOS, without a source: the system's broadcast picker is the picker, and the call completes once the user taps **Start Broadcast**. The capture runs in a Broadcast Upload Extension in the app, which the package ships as templates (`ios/broadcast_extension/`); the README's "iOS screen share setup" walks through adding the target, the App Group and the `Info.plist` keys.
  - The setup is checked before each share: if anything is missing, the share doesn't start and reports a `ScreenShareSetupException` with `problems` (`BroadcastSetupProblem`) and `guidance`.
  - The picker can't report a cancel, so a broadcast not started within `ScreenShareOptions.broadcastStartTimeout` (60 s) makes `start()` return `false`, as a cancelled picker does elsewhere. A broadcast stopped from the status bar or Control Center ends the share with `ScreenShareEndReason.userStopped`.
  - `ScreenShareOptions.broadcastScale` (0.5) sets how much the extension scales the screen down; `frameRate` caps its frames. Screen audio (`captureAudio`) isn't supported on iOS and is ignored.
  - `MediaBackend.broadcastExtension` (a new `BroadcastExtensionBackend`) is the seam for it. Custom `MediaBackend`s must implement it (return `null` where none is needed).
  - Example: a `BroadcastExtension` target built from the templates, and **Share screen** on iOS; `screen_share_test.dart` and the screen mode of `cross_device_test.dart` run on an iPhone (with a person to tap "Start Broadcast").
- **Screen share on Android** (`docs/design.md` §10, roadmap M9):
  - `ScreenShareSource.start()` and `LocalParticipant.publishScreen()` work on Android, without a source: the system's consent dialog is the picker. A cancelled dialog returns `false` (`publishScreen` throws a `MediaCaptureException`), like a cancelled browser picker.
  - The package's Android plugin runs the share under its own foreground service of type `mediaProjection`, started after consent and before the capture, as Android 14+ requires. Its manifest declares the service and the `FOREGROUND_SERVICE`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PROJECTION` and `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` permissions, which merge into the app. The notification has a **Stop sharing** action; the package asks for the notification permission once per launch on Android 13+, and the share works without it.
  - A share stopped from the system (the status-bar chip) or from the notification ends with `ScreenShareEndReason.userStopped`, and the room unpublishes it.
  - `ScreenShareSource.usesSystemPicker`: `true` on the web and Android, where the browser or the system picks what to share. `usesBrowserPicker` stays web-only.
  - `MediaBackend.screenCaptureService` (a new `ScreenCaptureServiceBackend`) is the seam for the consent dialog and the service. Custom `MediaBackend`s must implement it (return `null` where none is needed).
  - Screen audio (`captureAudio`) isn't supported on Android and is ignored.
  - Example: on Android, **Share screen** goes straight to the consent dialog; `integration_test/screen_share_test.dart`.
- The same camera behaviour on every platform:
  - **The front camera opens by default.** `CameraOptions.facing` now defaults to `CameraFacing.user`, and cameras facing that way are tried first (Android lists its back camera first, so the back camera used to open). Pass `facing: null` for the platform's order.
  - **`CameraSource.switchCamera()` and `LocalParticipant.switchCamera()`:** one call that flips front ↔ back on phones and moves to the next camera elsewhere (virtual cameras last). A published camera keeps its track and sender, so subscribers see the new camera without pulling again.
  - `MediaDevice.facing` says which way a camera faces, from the platform (Android, iOS) or the label (browsers); `CameraSource.currentFacing` for the camera in use. `CameraFacing` moved to `media_types.dart` (still exported from the package).
  - `ParticipantVideoView` no longer mirrors a back camera's self-view by default.
  - On iOS, the plain cameras come first: iOS lists its virtual multi-camera devices ("Back Triple Camera") first, and `flutter_webrtc` gets no frames from them, so switching to the back camera sent nothing. Apple's built-in cameras are now ordered by the index in their ID.
  - Example: a **Switch camera** button on the call screen, and `integration_test/camera_switch_test.dart`.
- The same call audio on every phone (`docs/design.md` §4.6):
  - **Audio routes:** `Room.audioRoutes`, `currentAudioRoute` / `audioRouteChanges` and `selectAudioRoute(route)`. `AudioRoute` has an `id`, a `kind` (`speaker`, `earpiece`, `wiredHeadset`, `bluetooth`, `usb`, `other`) and a `name`. The list is the same on Android and iOS, and shows only routes the platform will honour (no earpiece while Bluetooth is connected). The current route is read back from the platform. `canSelectAudioRoute` is `false` on desktops and the web.
  - **The default follows the call:** a voice call starts on the earpiece and moves to the speaker once the call has video (sent or received). Before, iOS used the earpiece even for video, while Android always used the speaker. A connected headset comes first, and a route the user picked sticks until a new headset connects or the route goes. `RoomOptions.speakerphone` (`null` by default) can force the speaker (`true`) or the earpiece (`false`); `Room.setSpeakerphone(on)` does the same mid-call, and `speakerphone` / `speakerphoneChanges` report it.
  - **The package is now a Flutter plugin**, with a small native layer on Android (`setCommunicationDevice` on Android 12+, speakerphone and Bluetooth SCO before that) and on iOS (AVFoundation). On Android it turns off flutter_webrtc's own audio routing (AudioSwitch), which re-picked a route on every device change; flutter_webrtc's Android list of audio outputs is empty as a result.
  - **Bluetooth on Android 12+** needs the app's `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT` permission (declared and requested at runtime); without it, Bluetooth routes are missing. The example asks for it before joining.
  - **Choosing a microphone works on Android.** The Android plugin ignored the microphone named in `getUserMedia` (it only reported it back), so `MicrophoneSource.setPreferredDevice` changed nothing; the backend now selects it first.
  - Example: an audio route button on the call screen (phones), with a sheet of routes; `integration_test/audio_routing_test.dart`.
- Example on iOS: an optional, git-ignored `ios/Flutter/Signing.xcconfig` for your signing team, and `NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription` for the dev server. `docs/checkpoint.md` covers running on an iPhone, and `--no-uninstall` for integration tests on phones (so permissions survive between runs).
- Fixes from the first runs against the real SFU (Windows):
  - Offers and answers no longer carry stray receive-only audio and video m-lines: native `flutter_webrtc` adds them unless `createOffer`/`createAnswer` get explicit empty constraints.
  - Negotiated DataChannels open on Windows: the plugin never reports their `open` state, so the package reads it from `getStats()`. Closing a session no longer logs `dataChannelClose() peerConnection is null`.
  - Unsubscribing or unpublishing the last track of a session no longer fails (`max-bundle configured but session description has no BUNDLE group`): those m-lines are closed with `force` and kept idle.
  - `setPreferredRid` right after a pull retries while the SFU isn't forwarding the track yet (`SfuSessionOptions.layerUpdateRetryTimeout`, 10 s).
  - On Windows and Linux, at most 8 ICE server URLs reach `flutter_webrtc` (its desktop plugin overflows a fixed array beyond that).
- Fixes from the first runs against the real SFU on Android (a Pixel 10, Android 16):
  - Publishing works on Android: every push failed with "the transceiver has no mid", because the Android plugin replaces a transceiver's ID with its mid after negotiation. The package now finds its transceiver by the sender's ID, and stops a negotiated transceiver through its current entry, which the iOS and macOS plugin (whose transceiver ID is the mid) needs too.
  - Android doesn't report negotiated DataChannels as open either; the `getStats()` fallback from the Windows fix covers it (no change needed). The 8-ICE-server limit stays desktop-only.
- Fixes from the first runs against the real SFU on macOS (a MacBook Pro, macOS 27):
  - The camera captures at the requested preset on macOS and iOS: the Darwin plugin ignored `width: {ideal: 1280}` (it reads `ideal` only as a string), so the camera opened at its smallest format with a frame rate of 0, and a MacBook sent 640×480 in two simulcast layers instead of 1280×720 in three. Native platforms now get bare numbers, which every plugin reads; Android, which never read `ideal` either, captured 1280×720 whatever the preset; on a Pixel 10, 360p and 540p presets now capture 640×360 and 1024×576. Browsers still get `{ideal: n}`.
  - The loopback, DataChannel echo and reconnect integration tests, and cross-device calls with Windows and with Android, pass with no other change.
- Example: `integration_test/cross_device_test.dart`, a call between two devices (such as Windows and Android) through the same SFU: media both ways and simulcast layer switching, checked in `getStats()`. `docs/checkpoint.md` explains how to run it, and how to reach the dev server from a USB-connected phone with `adb reverse`.

- Video defaults to VP8 on every platform (was Windows only): the SFU forwards each publisher's codec, so a Windows subscriber must never receive H.264 (flutter-webrtc #982). Opt out with `SfuSessionDefaults.videoCodecPreferences: const []`.

- Reconnection: the `Room` replaces a broken SFU session by itself (failed or long-disconnected peer connection, a 410 `session_error`, a network change while disconnected, a return from the background after 30 s). It republishes its tracks and DataChannels under the same names without restarting capture, announces the new session ID (retrying a failing signaling update), and pulls its subscriptions again from the publishers' current sessions. `RoomOptions.reconnect` (`ReconnectOptions`: backoff, trigger timings, stable period), `Room.reconnect()`, `Room.isReconnecting`, `RoomReconnectingEvent`/`RoomReconnectedEvent`/`RoomReconnectFailedEvent`, `ReconnectReason.manual`. Network changes come from an app-provided `NetworkChangeSource` (none by default; the core has no connectivity plugin), the lifecycle from `AppLifecycleSource` (`FlutterAppLifecycleSource` by default), both passed to `CloudflareRealtime`.
- `Room.debugSimulateConnectionFailure()` and `SfuSession.debugSimulateFailure()` (`PeerConnectionFailureKind.simulated`) for tests and demos of recovery.
- Example app: the call screen shows a reconnecting banner (and a "Reconnect" button after giving up), has a "simulate network drop" action, and passes a network-change source that polls interface addresses. A reconnection integration test runs against a real broker when `CF_REALTIME_BROKER_URL` is set.
- A "session gone" answer to a pull or a DataChannel subscribe no longer fails the subscriber's own session outright: the session first checks itself with `GET sessions/{id}`. If it is alive, only those pulls or subscriptions fail (`SfuTrackException`/`SfuDataChannelException` with `session_error`) and back off, so a peer's stale session in signaling no longer makes the room re-session again and again. `HttpBrokerClient` keeps a session's token after a `SessionGoneException`, until `forgetSession`.
- Remote audio plays on the web: each pulled audio track gets a hidden `<audio>` element. `Room.audioPlaybackBlocked`/`audioPlaybackBlockedChanges` report the browser's autoplay policy, and `Room.startAudio()` (from a user gesture) starts the blocked audio. `Room.setAudioOutputDevice()`/`canSelectAudioOutput` choose the speaker (`setSinkId` on the web). Native platforms are unchanged. New direct dependencies: `web` and `dart_webrtc` (both already used by `flutter_webrtc`).
- After a reconnection, the room stays `reconnecting` until the new peer connection is connected (when tracks or DataChannels are on it), instead of briefly showing `connected`, `connecting`, `connected`.
- Example app: a "Click to enable audio" banner when the browser blocks playback.

VP8 covers screen video too (tested).
